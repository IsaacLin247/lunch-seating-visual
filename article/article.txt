     Rotating Group Assignments with a Companion Guarantee:
     Structural Limits, Auditable Screening, and a Hybrid Solver
                                                 Isaac Lin
                                      MIT Lincoln Laboratory; SMU

                              First manuscript draft, September 2026


                                                 Abstract
         Repeated group assignment can combine a familiar companion with opportunities to meet
     other participants. A hard guarantee that each submitter shares a group with at least one
     named peer, however, can also enable coordinated submissions to force a coalition together. We
     formalize this directed-list assignment problem with capacities, eligibility states, and two kinds of
     meeting history. We establish NP-completeness of its general feasibility problem and distinguish
     structural forcing from global feasibility. An explicit seven-person cycle with a shared outside
     anchor defeats closure-only screening, including when the closure is large. We introduce a sound,
     bounded screening procedure that tests whether a candidate core can be separated after relaxing
     outside obligations; inconclusive solver outcomes remain unresolved. A four-stage heuristic
     combines construction, repair, simulated annealing, and guarded constraint programming, with
     independent validation of every retained chart. In a synthetic case study with 257 participants
     and alternating eligibility states, 10 paired population seeds yield a mean exactly-one-listed-peer
     rate of 99.64% and a mean increase of +2.46 distinct peers over a capacity-matched random
     baseline after 16 assignments. These outcomes are conditional on the specified generator and
     budgets. Published charts also reveal preferences: under a stated public list-size cap, 29.8%
     of submitted directed entries are logically forced in the reference run. Full traces, an attempt
     ledger, independent checks, and an interactive playback accompany the study. The procedure
     does not establish complete resistance to coordinated submissions, feasibility of every admitted
     input, or psychological and distributive benefits in a real population.

Keywords: group assignment; directed preferences; constraint programming; simulated annealing;
strategic submissions; privacy inference


1    Introduction
A rotating lunch assignment has two potentially competing aims: give participants access to a
familiar peer and create contact beyond an established circle. We study a concrete operational
version of these aims. A participant may submit a list of acceptable companions, and every retained
assignment must place that participant with at least one eligible listed peer. Among assignments
satisfying this requirement, the procedure discourages additional listed peers and repeated meetings.
A declared acceptable companion is an input to an optimization problem; the model does not
establish friendship quality, emotional security, or improved belonging.
    The guarantee changes the strategic possibilities. A directed cycle of one-name lists forces its
members to remain together. Increasing a minimum list length is insufficient when some names
become ineligible in a particular rotation, and even two eligible names can force a group through
a shared outside anchor. Rejecting every unusual list structure is also unjustified: structural
dependence can arise from sincere preferences. An operational system therefore needs explicit proof
conditions, an honest account of screening coverage, and a procedure for unresolved submissions.
    This paper makes four contributions. First, it specifies the assignment and history objectives
precisely and proves a general feasibility hardness result. Second, it develops structural counterex-
amples and a sound relaxation for detecting forced candidate cores, together with capacity-demand

stages are saved and independently checkable. Fourth, it evaluates that implementation on a
reproducible synthetic study, including coordinated submissions, computational budgets, network
structure, and information revealed by released charts. The accompanying website replays actual
saved solver stages; it does not claim to optimize a new instance in the browser.


2    Related work
Practical seating optimization has been studied with application-specific objectives and local search
[1]. Work on optimal seat arrangement analyzes how preference and seating graph structure affect
computational complexity [2]. Our feasible sets differ because a directed acceptable-companion
condition is mandatory, groups have exact capacities, eligibility changes across rotations, and
past meetings affect the objective. The social golfer problem provides a related repeated-grouping
model focused on avoiding repeated pairs [3]. Here repeated listed companionship is sometimes
unavoidable: with a fixed list of at most eight names and 16 guaranteed assignments, a submitter
must repeat a listed companion.
     The feasibility reduction uses the graph-factor framework of Kirkpatrick and Hell [4]. The
structural analysis connects to vertex-disjoint directed cycles [5] and partitions of directed graphs
under degree conditions [6]. Alon’s directly related High School Coalitions note studies the same
one-listed-companion condition without the present capacities and changing eligibility states [7].
It already uses the cycle-extension argument and distinguishes the strategic possibilities with
two versus three names. We treat these arguments as prior results. Our contribution is their
application to state-dependent assignment, an auditable targeted separation test, and the evaluated
implementation; we do not claim the general coalition phenomenon as new. The optimization
stages use the Metropolis acceptance rule and simulated annealing [8, 9], followed by OR-Tools
CP-SAT [10]. Its status distinctions are essential: UNKNOWN supplies neither a feasibility nor an
infeasibility certificate [11].

4     Feasibility and structural limits
4.1   General computational complexity
Theorem 1. Deciding whether a valid single-rotation chart exists is NP-complete, even with
one eligibility block, all capacities equal to three, mutual lists, and every participant submitting a
nonempty list.

Proof. Membership in NP follows by checking a proposed chart. Reduce from P3 -factor, the
NP-complete problem of partitioning the vertices of an undirected graph into three-vertex paths
[4]. If the source graph has an isolated vertex or its order is not divisible by three, its answer is
NO. Map it to a fixed NO assignment instance: the six vertices of a star K1,5 , mutual neighbor
lists, and two tables of size three. Every leaf must sit with the center, which is impossible.
     Otherwise create one participant per source vertex, make every graph edge a mutual list relation,
and use tables of size three. Every list is nonempty. A source P3 -factor supplies a chart in which
every participant has a listed tablemate. Conversely, each valid three-person table induces a graph
with no isolated vertex. Such a graph is a path or a triangle and contains a spanning P3 . Selecting
one path per table gives the required factor. The construction is polynomial.

    The preprocessing matters because an isolated source vertex would otherwise become a non-
submitter with no obligation. The theorem establishes hardness of the general model. It does not
establish hardness of every restricted four-name, six-or-seven-seat instance used below.

4.2   Closed sets and forcing
Definition 2. A set S ⊆ V is guarantee-closed in a fixed state if every i ∈ S ∩ U has at least
one eligible listed peer in S. It is self-contained if every eligible name of each such i lies in S. A
self-contained set is structurally coercive if it admits no partition into two guarantee-closed parts
each containing a submitter.

    Guarantee-closed means at least one internal acceptable peer, whereas self-contained means
all acceptable peers remain inside. The forcing and demand arguments below require the latter
condition.

Proposition 3. All submitters of a self-contained structurally coercive set must share a table in
every valid chart.

Proof. Intersect the set with each occupied table of a valid chart. Self-containment and the
guarantee make each nonempty intersection guarantee-closed. If submitters occupy at least two
tables, one intersection and the union of the others form a forbidden two-part partition.

     This is a sufficient forcing certificate, not a global feasibility certificate. A noncoercive set may
still be impossible to place because of capacities or other participants’ obligations. Likewise, the
ability to fit a forced group at one table does not certify the remaining population.


                                                       3
Proposition 4. A self-contained set S with S ∩ U ̸= ∅ is not structurally coercive if every vertex
of S ∩ U has at least three distinct eligible out-neighbors in S ∩ U .

Proof. The minimum-outdegree-three theorem gives two vertex-disjoint directed cycles [5]. We use
the extension argument described by Alon [7], adapted to the obligated subgraph. Start with the
two cycles as separate guarantee-closed sets. Repeatedly attach any remaining obligated vertex
with an edge into an existing set. When no attachment is possible, every remaining obligated vertex
has all its obligated out-neighbors among the remaining vertices; their union is guarantee-closed
and can be merged into the first set. Non-obligated vertices can be assigned freely. Both sets still
contain their original cycle.

    Three arbitrary names do not meet this hypothesis if some become ineligible or are outside the
obligated subgraph. The implementation applies the shortcut only when all tested vertices have
obligations and counts distinct neighbors.

4.3    Coordinated submissions and the shared-anchor counterexample
For odd g ≥ 3, the directed circulant with edges i → i + 1 and i → i + 2 modulo g is structurally
coercive. Any directed cycle contains at least ⌈g/2⌉ vertices: its positive steps sum to a multiple
of g and each step is at most two. Two disjoint cycles are therefore impossible. Two nonempty
guarantee-closed parts would each contain a directed cycle, giving a contradiction. For even g ≥ 4,
the two parity classes instead provide a split.
    Eligibility permits this construction despite a four-name minimum. Seven same-grade partici-
pants can list their next two coalition members and two participants from the other grade. In a
same-grade rotation the last two names disappear, forcing the seven together. A related six-person
attack uses an odd five-person core and a sixth participant dependent on it.

Proposition 5. Let a coalition C = {v0 , . . . , vg−1 } have effective lists Lvi = {vi+1 mod g , h} for a
common outside anchor h ∈
                        / C. Every valid chart seats all members of C together, regardless of h’s
list.

Proof. A table containing a coalition member but not h must contain that member’s successor and,
inductively, the entire cycle. If the coalition occupied two tables, at least one would exclude h, so
that table would already contain the whole coalition, a contradiction.

    For g = 7 and maximum capacity seven, the coalition captures a full table and h sits elsewhere.
Adding two opposite-grade names to each list satisfies the four-name rule while preserving the
same-grade argument. A successor closure that follows h’s outgoing names can become very large.
Even an unbounded test of that larger closure can find a split separating the intact coalition from
the background without separating any coalition members. Closure size alone is therefore not the
complete problem.

4.5   Demand and eligibility-specific capacities
Proposition 7. Consider a self-contained core with n1 submitters and n0 nonsubmitters, together
with submitting followers whose nonempty eligible lists lie entirely in the core. At most n0 + ⌊n1 /2⌋
tables can contain any of these participants in a valid chart. Their total number must not exceed
the sum of that many largest capacities in their admissible eligibility pool.

Proof. Every occupied follower table contains a core member. A table with no nonsubmitting core
member requires at least two submitting core members, because a submitting core member must
find a core peer. Tables with a nonsubmitting core member account for at most n0 tables; all
remaining tables account for at most ⌊n1 /2⌋. The capacity bound follows.

    For example, five mutually linked core submitters and ten followers require 15 seats but can
use at most two seven-seat tables. A grade-specific example has 13 senior core members and
29 followers, demanding 42 seats. Their six largest senior tables supply only 41 seats; using the
combined grade pools would incorrectly supply 42. If the appropriate pool is unavailable, the
implementation reports an unresolved check rather than borrowing another grade’s capacities.
    Before generating a year, the implementation also attempts a hard-constraint feasibility solve for
each distinct eligibility state. FEASIBLE or OPTIMAL supplies a witness; INFEASIBLE proves
impossibility; UNKNOWN leaves the question open. A subsequently constructed and independently
validated chart establishes feasibility for that state even if the preliminary solve was inconclusive.


5     Hybrid solution method
Screen-enabled inputs with certified returns, unresolved screening checks, or invalid lists require
review before scheduling. The procedure does not invent substitute preferences. Attack scenarios
with screening disabled are experiments on the guarantee’s strategic consequences. The screened
reference scenario explicitly replaces a returned six-person attack by a specified six-person star; it
is one modeled resubmission, not an optimal attacker response.

5.1   Construction and repair
Construction prioritizes participants with few supporting options, forms mutually compatible pods
when possible, and merges pods using compatibility and history scores without exceeding the
minimum capacity. Packing failures can require singleton placement. The constructed chart has
correct capacities but need not satisfy every companion obligation.
    Repair uses directed exchanges and compound moves that close supporting dependencies. It
attempts to reduce the unsatisfied set without orphaning a previously supported participant; a
compound repair is kept only when the prescribed violation and cost checks succeed. A failed local
repair does not imply infeasibility.

6    Information revealed by published charts
A public chart can reveal private list entries even when the lists themselves are hidden. We analyze
an observer who knows that participant i submitted a fixed list, knows a public upper bound K,
                                                                                  (r)
knows the guarantee was honored, and observes all published charts. Let Ti be the set of i’s
tablemates in rotation r. Every candidate list A must satisfy
                                                (r)
                           |A| ≤ K,      A ∩ Ti       ̸= ∅ for all observed r.                       (8)

A name v is forced if excluding v makes these hitting-set constraints impossible under the cap. An
exact dynamic program over covered-rotation bitmasks computes minimum hitting-set sizes. An
independent CP-SAT set-cover formulation checks the resulting exclusion certificates.
    The production inference uses the public cap, not the private true list length. The assumed-
public submitter identities are obtained from the input. Actual positive list entries are consulted
only afterward to score correctness and the fraction of directed entries recovered. Recovering every
true positive entry does not generally determine the entire list: additional names may still fit below
the public cap. A list is uniquely determined by forced positives only when those positives fill the
public cap, or fill an independently known exact list length. Exhausting the possible peer universe
is another sufficient uniqueness condition; this degenerate case does not arise in the study. The
output distinguishes complete true-positive recovery from uniquely determined lists.
    Under this observer model, no individual name is forced in the first K rotations when every
observed table has at least three members. After excluding any one name, each tablemate set
remains nonempty; choosing one remaining peer per rotation gives a hitting set of size at most
R ≤ K. This explains the initial flat portion of the leakage curve. It does not mean that charts
reveal no information: they already restrict the set of possible lists even without forcing an individual
entry.
    This observer model omits some possible side information. Additional eligibility or input-rule
knowledge can strengthen deductions; uncertainty about submission or compliance can weaken
them. These calculations are therefore conditional logical implications under stated knowledge, not
a universal privacy-loss metric. The system provides no formal privacy guarantee, and the study
does not evaluate a privacy-preserving replacement.

7     Experimental design
7.1    Synthetic population and admission policy
The reference population has 257 participants: 132 in grade 11 and 125 in grade 12. Forty tables
contain 17 groups of seven and 23 groups of six. Same-grade rotations allocate twelve seven-seat
and eight six-seat tables to grade 11, and five seven-seat and fifteen six-seat tables to grade 12.
Sixteen rotations alternate mixed-grade and same-grade states, starting mixed.
    A directed synthetic network targets up to eight true nominations per participant, using
lognormal popularity, a within-grade weight, and reciprocal-edge generation. The reference regime
reaches eight names; small latent groups at µ = 1 can yield shorter lists. Optional latent groups alter
within-group preference and overlap. These parameters are scenario assumptions, not estimates
from a school network. A six-person mutual clique is planted for attack comparisons, within one
junior latent group when groups are enabled. The construction preserves within-group isolation
at µ = 1, ω = 0. The shared-anchor experiment adds a deterministic seventh coalition member
and changes submitted lists while retaining the underlying population. Honest participants whose
generated lists fail the admission rule become nonsubmitters; names are not padded in this study.
Thus the guarantee’s denominator is the admitted submitter set, and admission exclusions must be
reported separately from seating violations. In the retained study every participant submitted an
accepted list and there were no rule-based exclusions; this study therefore supplies no outcomes for
actually excluded participants.

7.4   Study plan, evidence, and uncertainty
The primary comparison uses population seeds 1–10, each with honest and omission-star scenarios
at the default budget and 16 rotations. Four additional seed-seven scenarios supply the remaining
website references. Sensitivity runs vary annealing budgets to 100,000 and 900,000 iterations, CP
budgets to 0.1 and 1.0 deterministic-time units, horizons to 5 and 32 rotations, and latent-group
settings to (µ, ω) = (0.6, 0.3) and (1, 0). Each sensitivity changes its named setting relative to the
seed-seven honest reference; it is not a factorial exploration. Population seeds and solver seeds are
separate recorded fields.
    Every attempted run is written to an atomic ledger before execution. Successful rows retain
complete chart traces, effective inputs, configuration, source-file hashes, dependency versions, the
full Git commit, and dirty-tree status. Interrupted, invalid, failed, and unresolved attempts remain
visible. Resumption requires matching effective source and configuration identity and verified trace
hashes. Table 2 reports actual attempts, including interruptions. Historical summary-only results
are retained separately and are excluded from the new numerical tables.
    Population seeds, rather than students or rotations, are the independent units for paired
comparisons. Reported ranges and a 95% paired-population bootstrap percentile interval (20,000
resamples, PCG64 seed 20260908, linear sample quantiles) describe variation under this generator,
conditional on the budgets and fixed study design; they are not uncertainty intervals for a real
school population. Runtime measurements were collected with concurrent experiments on a shared
machine and are not isolated hardware benchmarks.

8     Results
8.1   Guarantee satisfaction and contact diversity
All successful charts included in the results pass independent checks of placement, capacities,
eligibility, and the companion guarantee. This is a statement about retained, validated outputs; it
is not evidence that every rule-compliant input is feasible or that every attempt succeeds. Across
10 honest population seeds, mean exactly-one coverage is 99.64%, with population means ranging
from 99.44% to 99.73%. The paired change in final distinct peers averages +2.46, ranging from
+2.13 to +3.19; the conditional 95% bootstrap interval for the mean is [2.26, 2.70].
    For the seed-seven honest reference, mean exactly-one coverage is 99.68%, and its minimum
rotation value is 98.44%. Mean final distinct peers are 74.44 under the proposed schedule and
72.31 under the paired random schedule. Figure 3 shows the full trajectory, while Figure 4 shows
population-level variation. The proposed schedule has more repeated pairs than its random
comparator in 3 reference rotations (3, 5, 7). An aggregate diversity improvement therefore does
not imply a per-rotation dominance result, and exactly-one coverage is an optimization statistic
rather than a measure of social benefit.

8.3                     Budget and network sensitivity
Table 6 and Figure 5 vary computational budgets, horizon, and latent network structure. A larger
budget changes a finite search opportunity; it need not monotonically improve every downstream-
year metric. The µ = 1, ω = 0 latent-group setting has exactly-one coverage 89.88%, compared with
99.68% in the reference. Network concentration can therefore substantially change the apparent
ability to minimize extras. At µ = 1, smaller groups also shorten realized lists (mean 7.07 names
rather than eight), so this comparison changes both network concentration and the list-length
distribution. It is not an isolated causal estimate of community structure. One-population sensitivity
runs identify dependence on assumptions but cannot establish general robustness.

8.4                    Privacy implications and distributional observations
In the reference run, the public-cap inference forces 613 of 2056 directed submitted entries (29.8%),
affecting 243 participants. Comparison with private synthetic truth confirms 613 forced entries as
correct. The inference classifies 0 lists as uniquely determined under the public cap; this count is
separate from complete recovery of a shorter true list. Figure 6 displays the accumulation of forced
entries and the distribution of distinct contacts.
     The 32-rotation sensitivity forces 1691 directed entries (82.2%) and uniquely determines 71
lists under the public cap. The five-rotation probe forces no entries, as predicted by the preceding
hitting-set argument. These are one-population horizon comparisons under fixed submissions; they
do not imply that every population has the same disclosure rate.
     Individual final distinct-peer counts range from 64 to 82. Participants in the lowest and
highest incoming-nomination quartiles average 75.78 and 72.48 distinct peers, respectively. These
descriptive group means neither demonstrate equal treatment nor establish that nomination degree
caused an outcome. They omit other dimensions of fairness, including satisfaction quality and the
burden on participants with few acceptable peers.

9    Discussion and limitations
The strongest guarantees in this work are local and explicit: every released chart satisfies the stated
constraints, and every certified targeted core is forced together under the relaxation’s hypotheses.
Neither statement implies that the screen detects every forceable coalition. Single-anchor deletion
and a bounded core size target useful counterexamples, but multiple anchors, larger cores, and
adaptive submissions remain outside an exhaustive analysis. A screen passage means only that
the implemented checks did not certify a problem. A proved-coercive structure does not establish
dishonest intent.
    Global feasibility remains a distinct question. Minimum list length and two same-grade names
do not imply it, and a time-limited unknown solve must not be described as a proof of failure.
Where an instance needs review, an operational deployment would need a transparent process
involving participants or administrators; this study does not validate such a process. Turning short
generated lists into nonsubmission is a documented simulation policy, not evidence that excluding
real participants would be acceptable.
    The objectives are operational proxies. Fewer extra listed peers and more distinct contacts may
be useful design choices, but this study contains no intervention, calibrated friendship network,
or measured wellbeing outcome. The synthetic generator, planted coalition, public cap, and
fixed budgets restrict external validity. The random baseline measures a particular unconstrained
alternative; it does not establish superiority over every feasible heuristic. The study also lacks a
full-year optimality certificate and extensive algorithmic ablations.
    Publication of charts has a substantive privacy cost in the observer model. Hiding list lengths
does not eliminate deductions when a public cap and repeated guarantee are known. The project
should not be presented as privacy-preserving. Any future deployment would need a separately
specified data policy and a study of mitigations and their effect on the guarantee.
    Source and input fingerprints improve auditability, but multiworker optimization and platform
differences can still change search trajectories. The attempt ledger and independently validated
trace are the evidence for each reported outcome. Historical proposal files describing maximization
of two-friend coverage or all-mixed baselines are not the current specification. The original external
prototype and collusion-result files described in earlier materials were not available in the repository
used for this revision; no claim of reproducing those historical experiments is made.


10     Conclusion
A hard companion guarantee creates both an assignment constraint and a strategic dependency
structure. Shared outside anchors show why closure-only reasoning can miss full-table capture. A
targeted relaxation yields sound forcing certificates for examined cores, while explicit unresolved
states prevent time limits from masquerading as proofs. The implemented hybrid solver produces
independently validated synthetic schedules with high exactly-one coverage and modest gains in
distinct contacts relative to a matched random baseline. Those gains coexist with detectable
preference disclosure and unresolved questions about complete screening, real-world feasibility, and
social outcomes.

B     Regression evidence and audit scope
The regression suite covers the 257-participant shared-anchor witness, restoration of anchor edges
during targeted testing, exact senior capacity pools, and unknown solver statuses. Exhaustive
seating partitions of 180 small random graphs independently check that every certified core is forced
together. These tests validate soundness on the enumerated cases; they do not prove completeness
of candidate generation. The star enumeration checks all restricted four-of-five wirings and retains
all feasible partition patterns. Privacy tests distinguish a recovered short list from a uniquely
determined list under a public cap. Ledger tests preserve interrupted attempts, reject stale or
modified traces, and bind resumption to matching source and configuration identity.
    The accompanying audit-resolution document maps each follow-up finding to its correction or
remaining limitation. Historical source documents and summary-only experiments are preserved as
history. Their former objective and numbers are excluded from current claims rather than silently
treated as fresh evidence.
