    How it works

    Constraints first, then better seating

    A validated heuristic, with explicit limits

    A listed peer is a directed submission, not proof of mutual friendship. Let \(L_i^{(q)}\) contain all listed names in mixed rounds and only same-grade names in same-grade rounds; \(s_i\) counts these eligible names at the table. Empty submissions receive seats but owe no friend constraint.

    1. Classify and screenSubmission states, eligible graphs, bounded coercion tests, full-model separation tests, grade-specific demand
    2. Certify and cacheHard CP-SAT model per set of constraints; a validated witness becomes a fallback; infeasibility and unknown stay distinct
    3. Build and repairGreedy pods, compatible tables, guarded rescue swaps
    4. ImproveAnnealing, then hinted CP-SAT on the exact objective within a fixed budget
    5. Validate and releaseRelease only a validated chart, optimized or fallback; update pair histories for the next round

    These are four snapshots recorded during the honest run. Early stages can violate the friend guarantee. Animated transitions connect recorded assignments; intervening search moves are not recorded.

    1. Hard requirements

    Binary \(x_{i,t}\) assigns every student once, fills every table, and forbids incompatible grades. For each submitter, binary \(y_{i,t}\) can certify an eligible tablemate.

    \[ \sum_t x_{i,t}=1,\qquad \sum_i x_{i,t}=c_t,\qquad x_{i,t}=0\ \text{if the grade is incompatible} \]
    \[ y_{i,t}\le x_{i,t},\qquad y_{i,t}\le\sum_{j\in L_i^{(q)}}x_{j,t},\qquad \sum_t y_{i,t}\ge1 \]

    A feasible chart proves that these constraints can be satisfied. A solver status of UNKNOWN proves neither feasibility nor impossibility. A witness found here is validated independently and kept as a fallback for every rotation with the same hard constraints: roster, obligations, eligibility, occupancy targets, and staff constraints. Meeting history changes scores, not validity.

    2. Greedy construction and repair

    Let \(U\) be the currently unseated students. The next seed has few eligible unseated choices. Closed pods are packed into compatible tables. Repair preserves every already-satisfied submitter and reduces the friendless set \(V\); it may stop before finding feasibility.

    \[ i^*=\arg\min_{i\in U}|L_i^{(q)}\cap U|,\qquad V_{\mathrm{after}}\subsetneq V_{\mathrm{before}}\ \text{for accepted rescue moves} \]

    3. Annealing across full history

    Minimize this energy over capacity-preserving, grade-compatible group swaps. Reject moves that increase the number of friendless submitters; padded groups are re-closed. Histories are disjoint: \(m_{ab}\) counts incidental meetings, and \(\alpha_{ab}\) counts meetings while either student listed the other.

    \[ C=1000|V|+\sum_{i:\,L_i\ne\varnothing}\max(0,s_i-1)+\frac1{10}\sum_{\{a,b\}\text{ co-seated}}(5m_{ab}+2\alpha_{ab}) \]
    \[ P(\text{accept uphill})=e^{-\Delta C/T},\qquad T:2.5\longrightarrow0.02 \]

    One listed peer is a hard minimum. Exactly one and new acquaintances are optimization preferences, not individual promises. The weight of the extra-peer term is configurable; the default is one.

    4. Bounded CP-SAT on the exact objective

    The better of the annealed chart and the validated incumbent supplies a complete hint. Here \(e_i\) is an integer variable counting listed peers beyond the first, and \(r_{ab}\) marks a repeat for every pair with nonzero history \(H\), not only pairs from the previous rotation.

    \[ \min\ 10\sum_i e_i+\sum_{\{a,b\}\in H}(5m_{ab}+2\alpha_{ab})r_{ab} \]

    For every valid chart this equals ten times the annealing objective, so the two stages agree. A returned chart is rescored independently and replaces a valid incumbent only if it is valid and no worse. If every stage fails, the validated fallback chart is released and labelled as such; a chart is called optimal only when CP-SAT proved it. Nothing invalid is ever released.

    What screens establish

    Checks use each rotation’s eligible graph and admissible grade capacities. High-risk candidates also get a full-model separation test: with the complete assignment model, can the group occupy two or more tables? A validated separating chart, a forcing certificate under the current constraints, or an unresolved result are the three outcomes. A proven coercive candidate or capacity shortage requires intervention. An unresolved bounded search requires review or retry. Passing tested candidates is neither a complete anti-collusion guarantee nor a feasibility certificate, and a forced group is not evidence of intent.

    What charts reveal

    Repeated charts can reveal submitted names, even when collection is confidential.

    The inference probe assumes a known submitter set, fixed lists, a public list cap, and access to every chart. It proves necessary names under those assumptions; it does not show that unlisted tablemates are strangers. Distributing one chart at a time and removing a public cap remove logical forcing but not statistical inference, so no such measure guarantees confidentiality.

    Trace provenance and study scope

    These are synthetic examples, not a school trial or a calibrated model of friendships. Outcome rates do not establish fairness, student benefit, complete attack resistance, or success on arbitrary inputs. The paper reports the actual completed experiment inventory. Student-facing distribution uses a chart-only export; the full trace below is a staff record and is public here only because every student is synthetic.
