Constraints first, then better seating

A validated heuristic, with explicit limits

A listed peer is a directed submission, not proof of mutual friendship. Let \(L_i^{(q)}\) contain all listed names in mixed rounds and only same-grade names in same-grade rounds; \(s_i\) counts these eligible names at the table. Empty submissions receive seats but owe no friend constraint.

  1. Check submissionsRules, eligible graphs, bounded coercion tests, grade-specific demand
  2. Check feasibilityHard CP-SAT model; distinguish a witness, infeasibility, and unknown
  3. Build and repairGreedy pods, compatible tables, guarded rescue swaps
  4. ImproveAnnealing, then hinted CP-SAT polishing within a fixed budget
  5. Validate and recordExport only a valid chart; update pair histories for the next round

1. Hard requirements

Binary \(x_{i,t}\) assigns every student once, fills every table, and forbids incompatible grades. For each submitter, binary \(y_{i,t}\) can certify an eligible tablemate.

\[ \sum_t x_{i,t}=1,\qquad \sum_i x_{i,t}=c_t,\qquad x_{i,t}=0\ \text{if the grade is incompatible} \]
\[ y_{i,t}\le x_{i,t},\qquad y_{i,t}\le\sum_{j\in L_i^{(q)}}x_{j,t},\qquad \sum_t y_{i,t}\ge1 \]

A feasible chart proves that these constraints can be satisfied. A solver status of UNKNOWN proves neither feasibility nor impossibility.

2. Greedy construction and repair

Let \(U\) be the currently unseated students. The next seed has few eligible unseated choices. Closed pods are packed into compatible tables. Repair preserves every already-satisfied submitter and reduces the friendless set \(V\); it may stop before finding feasibility.

\[ i^*=\arg\min_{i\in U}|L_i^{(q)}\cap U|,\qquad V_{\mathrm{after}}\subsetneq V_{\mathrm{before}}\ \text{for accepted rescue moves} \]

3. Annealing across full history

Minimize this energy over capacity-preserving, grade-compatible group swaps. Reject moves that increase the number of friendless submitters; padded groups are re-closed. Histories are disjoint: \(m_{ab}\) counts incidental meetings, and \(\alpha_{ab}\) counts meetings while either student listed the other.

\[ C=1000|V|+\sum_{i:\,L_i\ne\varnothing}\max(0,s_i-1)+\frac1{10}\sum_{\{a,b\}\text{ co-seated}}(5m_{ab}+2\alpha_{ab}) \]
\[ P(\text{accept uphill})=e^{-\Delta C/T},\qquad T:2.5\longrightarrow0.02 \]

One listed peer is a hard minimum. Exactly one and new acquaintances are optimization preferences, not individual promises.

4. Bounded CP-SAT polishing

The annealed chart supplies a complete hint, which need not be feasible. Here \(z_i\) marks two or more listed peers, and \(r_{ab}\) marks a repeat for pairs from the previous rotation \(P_{q-1}\).

\[ \min\ 300\sum_i z_i+100\sum_{\{a,b\}\in P_{q-1}}(5m_{ab}+2\alpha_{ab})r_{ab} \]

This surrogate counts affected students rather than every extra peer and considers only previous-round pairs. Accept a polished chart only if it satisfies all guarantees and does not worsen the full-history score. A fallback feasibility solve can run; if no valid chart is found, report failure and export no seating.

What screens establish

Checks use each rotation’s eligible graph and admissible grade capacities. A proven coercive candidate or capacity shortage requires intervention. An unresolved bounded search requires review or retry. Passing tested candidates is neither a complete anti-collusion guarantee nor a feasibility certificate.

What charts reveal

Repeated charts can reveal submitted names, even when collection is confidential.

The inference probe assumes a known submitter set, fixed lists, a public list cap, and access to every chart. It proves necessary names under those assumptions; it does not show that unlisted tablemates are strangers.

Trace provenance and study scope

These are synthetic examples, not a school trial or a calibrated model of friendships. Outcome rates do not establish fairness, student benefit, complete attack resistance, or success on arbitrary inputs. The paper reports the actual completed experiment inventory.
