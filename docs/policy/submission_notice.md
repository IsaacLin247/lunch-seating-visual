# Draft submission notice (for review by the school before any use)

This is a draft. It has not been approved by the school, reviewed by legal counsel, or shown to students. It describes what the seating system does with a submitted list so that a school can decide whether, and how, to use it.

## What you are asked to submit

You may give the school a list of four to eight schoolmates you would be comfortable sitting with at lunch, including at least two from your own grade. You may also submit no list. Submitting no list is a normal choice and does not affect how often you are seated or where.

Your list is a request, not a promise about friendship. Naming someone does not tell them, and does not require them to name you.

## What the system promises

When a valid chart exists, every student who submitted a list is seated with at least one person from that list at every lunch, as long as that person is present that day and eligible for the round (in same-grade rounds, only same-grade names count). This is the only promise. Being seated with exactly one listed person, or with new people, is an optimisation goal, not a guarantee.

If your list has fewer than four names, or fewer than two same-grade names, it is not discarded. It is held as a request for review. A member of staff will either approve it as an exception, so it keeps its promise, ask you to add names, or record that you withdrew it. Nobody will add names on your behalf.

If, on a given day, every person on your list is absent or ineligible, the promise cannot be kept for that day. Staff will decide how to handle that day and will record the decision; the system will not quietly drop your request.

## What stays private, and what does not

Your list is stored with your submission and is seen only by the staff who run the process. It is not printed on the seating chart, not shown to other students, and not included in anything distributed to students.

However, the seating charts themselves reveal information. Anyone who sees several charts and knows the promise above can work out that certain people must be on your list, because you were seated with them repeatedly. Over a year of published charts, a substantial share of list entries can be deduced this way. Publishing one chart at a time, and not keeping an archive, reduces what can be deduced but does not remove it: the person you sit with most often is, in most cases, someone from your list. Please treat your list as something that will be partly visible to attentive observers through the charts, and do not include a name you would not want associated with you.

## Who can see the charts

The chart for each lunch is distributed to students. The school decides how (paper, a notice board, a school system, or a website). A public website can be seen by anyone, including people outside the school. The school should not publish charts on an unrestricted website if it wants to limit who can make the deductions above.

## Your choices

- Submit a list of four to eight names, or no list.
- Ask staff to withdraw or change your list at any time before a rotation is scheduled; changes take effect from the next rotation.
- Ask staff which of your listed names, if any, was used to seat you in a given rotation.

## Questions

Direct questions to the member of staff named by the school as responsible for lunch seating. This notice will be updated if the process changes.
